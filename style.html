Milestone 2 · drawing language

Blueprint — accuracy as the style

Four props covering the range: a curved body, a large panel with screen content, fine repeated detail, and cylinders in depth. Every object is built in true 3D coordinates and projected, so the geometry is measurably correct rather than eyeballed — which is the whole point of a blueprint.

Corrected from the first pass. I had built this with a baked hand-wobble to read as sketched. That was the wrong reading — the wobble is gone and the tool that produced it is deleted. What replaces it is real drafting: centre lines, hidden edges as dashes, extension and dimension lines, and a construction grid.

Ground

Same drawing, two grounds. Light keeps the site's near-white page and reads as a technical drawing. True blueprint is the classic reversed print — stronger as an idea, but it forces every section it appears in to go dark.

Line weights

Outline2.4
Visible edge1.5
Hidden edge1.1
Construction0.9

Drafting convention: outline heaviest, visible edges medium, hidden and construction lightest. The hierarchy is what makes it read as a drawing rather than an outline.

Drawing elements

X-ray is the purest wireframe — every edge visible, nothing solid. Beautiful on one object; on a thirty-object desk it becomes unreadable, which is why the scene keeps solid fills and shows hidden edges only on the object in shot.

Check

Zoom1.0×

Turn the stroke setting off and zoom: lines thicken with the camera and the precision is lost. On means a 3× zoom shows more detail at the same weight — exactly what shots 4 and 7 need.

What to judge. Does it read as measured? Blueprint lives or dies on whether the geometry looks true — ellipses correct for the viewing angle, edges meeting exactly, centre lines where the axes actually are.

Then: how much annotation is right? Dimensions and centre lines on every one of thirty objects would be noise. My instinct is that the resting desk carries grid and centre lines only, and the object in shot earns its dimensions and hidden edges as the camera arrives — annotation as another thing that resolves on focus, alongside colour.
