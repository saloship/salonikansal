Same keyboard, two pipelines — and why the left one won

Both at 368×130 mm, both in the scene's cabinet projection, both drawn with the identical line-weight hierarchy, both built from the same spec. The left is hand-authored SVG. The right is modelled in Blender from real ANSI dimensions and rendered through Freestyle with computed hidden-line removal.

The right one is more truthful and reads worse. A keyboard is a flat array of 87 near-identical small parts, and at any zoom this object is actually seen at, its truth is sub-pixel — the real 1.05 mm gap between caps is a fifth of a pixel. Every faithful edge the bake adds (taper sides, cap fronts, hidden rims) lands as noise rather than information, and it stops reading as a keyboard. So the keyboard is hand-authored, recorded as "pipeline": "hand" in its spec.

The bake still earns its place, but on different objects: the lamp's articulated arm, the laptop hinge, the scissor riser, the chair, and curved bodies. There, computed occlusion and exact curvature are information that cannot be had by hand. The registration transform below is printed by the builder, never tuned — a bake drops into the real drawing unchanged.
